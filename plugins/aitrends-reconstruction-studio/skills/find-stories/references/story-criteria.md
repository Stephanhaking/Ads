# What makes a strong reconstruction story

## Scoring rubric (1–5 each; weighted total out of 100)
| # | Criterion | Weight | 5 looks like | 1 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Open question** | 18 | One sharp, unresolved "how/why" that the film can structure around ("How did a rider come out of a bar built to lock?") | Fully explained; nothing to investigate |
| 2 | **Reconstructable in 3D** | 16 | A precise place and a physical sequence or mechanism (cockpit, ride, bridge, ship, crossing, stadium gate, lock, door, sensor) | Abstract (financial fraud, online abuse) or no known layout |
| 3 | **Source strength** | 16 | Official statements + named-outlet reporting + documents (filings, inspection reports, patents, flight data) | Social posts and one outlet |
| 4 | **Eyewitness voices** | 12 | Several named witnesses quoted by outlets, ideally in different positions (inside / outside / officials) | No first-hand accounts |
| 5 | **Timeline precision** | 10 | Clock times available (tracking data, police logs, statements) | Only "on Monday" |
| 6 | **Freshness & interest** | 12 | Last 7–14 days, still developing, widely searched; or an evergreen case with new findings | Old and settled with no new angle |
| 7 | **Precedents / context** | 8 | Comparable past cases with official findings, rules or patents that explain the system | None |
| 8 | **Sensitivity (inverse)** | 8 | Adults, officially named or not needed; no graphic material required | Story centres on a minor, graphic footage, or an unnamed private suspect |

Score = Σ(score × weight) / 5. Shortlist: ≥ 70 strong, 55–69 workable, < 55 only if the user insists.

## Hard rules (drop the candidate)
- **Unconfirmed:** the event isn't confirmed by at least two independent reports or one official source.
- **Danger to people:** an active manhunt or ongoing hostage situation, where a film could endanger people or prejudice an operation.
- **A minor at the centre:** the core of the story is a minor's private life or a minor's identity.
- **Graphic main source:** the only primary material is graphic footage of a death or injury.
- **Defamation risk:** the open question can only be answered by speculating about a named private person's guilt.

## Search recipes (combine; run in parallel)
- **Incident terms:**
  - `"investigation" incident <area> this week`
  - `"what we know" <area>`
  - `"witnesses describe"`
  - `"emergency landing"`
  - `"ride closed" OR "temporarily closed similar"`
  - `collapse investigation`
  - `"preliminary report"`
  - `NTSB OR AAIB OR BEA preliminary`
  - `recall investigation`
  - `"evacuated" stuck hours`
- **By area:**
  - aviation: diversion, decompression, cockpit, runway incursion, hard landing, turbulence injuries;
  - parks: coaster, drop tower, restraint, lap bar, evacuation;
  - structures: bridge, crane, balcony, stadium, escalator, elevator;
  - maritime: ferry, cargo ship, bridge strike, capsized;
  - rail: derailment, signal, level crossing;
  - events: crowd crush, gate, barrier;
  - security: cockpit door, perimeter breach;
  - weather and disaster: flash flood timeline, wildfire escape.
- **Freshness:** add the month and year ("October 2026") or "this week"; sort by recency in extended mode.
- **Depth checks:** search the official side, with `site:` queries on the regulator, police, operator or ministry; and the document side: `filetype:pdf <incident>`, inspection reports, patents for the mechanism (Google Patents), court filings.

## Title patterns that suit the style
- What Really Happened Inside the <place>
- The <Thing> That <did the unexpected thing>
- <Object>: <the rule it broke> ("Lap Bars Should NEVER Open")
- How <a safety system> <failed / let someone in / let go>
- <Number> <Minutes/Feet/Seconds> Inside <place>

Keep titles factual: no claims the film can't support, no names of private individuals.

## Format fit
- **Long-form** needs enough sourced material for 12–18 minutes: a setting, a minute-by-minute event, an aftermath, an investigation and a status.
- **Short:** one clear moment, one mechanism and one open question.
- Many stories give one long film plus 1–3 shorts (the mechanism, the key quote, the status).
