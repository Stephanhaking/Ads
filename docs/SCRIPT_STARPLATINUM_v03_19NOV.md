## Cold open (0:00): 7:12 a.m.
[a bedroom in the morning light; a glass slides off a nightstand; a pale blue-and-purple arm]
You wake up at 7:12 and the world is slow. A glass slides off your nightstand and falls like it's underwater.
You don't reach for it. Something else does.
A huge pale arm, blue and purple, comes out of your shoulder and puts the glass back on the table before your own hand has even moved.
You've woken up with Star Platinum, Jotaro's Stand from JoJo's Bizarre Adventure.
And before tonight, it's going to cost you something you can't replace.

## 7:40 a.m.: what it is (0:35)
[the kitchen; your mum; a wardrobe]
Nobody else can see it. Your mum walks in and asks why you're staring at the wardrobe.
In the story this is called a Stand, and Star Platinum's base powers are simple: strength, speed and precision, all far beyond a human.
You think "left" and it's already moved.
Lesson one: you're not the strong one. Something next to you is.

## 12:05 p.m.: the supermarket (1:00)
[a supermarket; a tower of tinned tomatoes; a trolley clips it]
At noon you go out for bread, because you're trying to act normal.
A kid's trolley clips a tower of tinned tomatoes, two metres tall, and it starts to fall.
You flinch, and Star Platinum catches all of it. Forty tins, back on the shelf, labels out.
Then it keeps going. It straightens the shelf next to it. Then the pasta display.
The manager watches forty tins fly back into place all by themselves, and you leave without any bread.
Lesson two: it doesn't know when to stop. You have to tell it.

## 6 p.m.: the gym (1:35)
[an empty gym; one bar loaded with every plate in the building]
That evening you take it to an empty gym.
One of the heaviest deadlifts ever recorded is about five hundred and ten kilograms, lifted by Hafthor Bjornsson in twenty twenty-five. A human at the very edge.
You load every plate in the building onto one bar.
Star Platinum picks it up with one hand, like a shopping bag. The bar bends in the middle like a banana.
The owner walks in, looks at the bar, looks at you, and cancels your membership on the spot.
Lesson three: strength is easy. Putting things down gently is the hard part.
You get home and your hands are shaking, and you notice something: everything it does, you feel afterwards.

## Day 3: the barrage (2:25)
[a boxing gym; a heavy bag; sand running across the floor]
Every fan knows what comes next. ORA ORA ORA.
So you try it on a heavy bag. Real boxers' punches move at roughly six to eleven metres per second.
Now say, just as an example, that a Stand throws twenty punches a second at that speed.
The bag lasts four seconds. Then it splits, and sand pours across the floor like an hourglass.
You sit down in it. You're not hungry. You're starving. You eat three dinners that night.
You've started calling it Gary. It doesn't answer to Gary.
Lesson four: every punch has to be paid for, and you're the one who pays.

## Day 6: Star Finger (3:00)
[a street at night; keys falling through a drain grate]
At night your keys fall through a drain grate.
In the story, Star Platinum has a move called Star Finger: it stretches two of its fingers to reach further than its arms can.
Two fingers slide down between the bars like chopsticks, and come back up with your keys, a two-euro coin, and somebody's wedding ring.
You hand the ring in at the police station. You don't explain how you got it.
Lesson five: the useful tricks are the small ones.

## Day 10: the world notices (3:30)
[a park; a boy with a phone; a ball stopped in the air]
A boy in the park films you catching his football before it hits a pram.
In the video you're standing still, and the ball just stops in the air, a metre from the baby.
Some people say it's edited. Some people say it's a ghost.
Nobody says "Stand", because the only people who know what that is think it's a joke.
Lesson six: the ones who'd understand are the ones who'd never believe it.

## Day 15: how fast is fast enough (4:05)
[a whiteboard in your room; numbers, no weapons]
Fans love the scenes where Star Platinum catches bullets. So you ask how fast is fast enough.
You don't test it. You never would. You do it on a whiteboard.
Human visual reaction time is about a quarter of a second, between one hundred and ninety and two hundred and eighty-five milliseconds.
A nine-millimetre pistol bullet leaves the barrel at around three hundred and fifty to four hundred and ten metres per second.
Our own maths: at three hundred and sixty, it crosses ten metres in about twenty-eight milliseconds. That's about nine times shorter than your reaction time.
Usain Bolt, the fastest human ever measured, averaged about ten metres per second.
You put the marker down.
There's one thing in the story that doesn't depend on speed at all. Near the end of Part three, Jotaro learns to stop time, about five seconds at first.
You don't have that. You think.
Lesson seven: being fast doesn't make you fast enough for everything.

## Day 30: the job nobody asked you to do (5:00)
[a café; a tray of glasses tipping; you catching it without looking up]
By day thirty you can't stop catching things. Cups. Phones. A tray of glasses. A toddler falling off a wall.
Your shoulders are always tight. You watch the edges of every table.
Your friends notice you never relax. Your hands shake every evening.
Lesson eight: when you can catch everything, you start feeling responsible for everything.

## Day 40: the van (5:30)
[a hill; a delivery van rolling with nobody inside; a school crossing]
On day forty, a delivery van rolls down a hill with nobody in it.
At the bottom there's a school crossing, and the lollipop lady has just stepped out.
You run. You're not going to make it. Star Platinum isn't going to make it either.
So you do the only thing you've never tried. You hold your breath.
The world goes silent. The van hangs in the air like a photograph. The birds stop mid-wing.
You reach the van. Star Platinum puts both hands on the bonnet and turns it toward a hedge.
You step back. Your lungs burn. That's all you get: one breath.
And the world starts again. The van hits the hedge at walking speed. Nobody is hurt.
The lollipop lady blinks at you, and keeps holding her sign.

## Day 41: the cost (6:20)
[your room; the figure behind you; you, exhausted]
The next morning you can't lift your hands, and they don't stop shaking until lunch.
Now you know what it really is. Not a superpower. A second body that never gets tired, attached to one that does.
Lesson nine: the strongest thing about you is still the most tired thing about you.

## Ending (6:45)
[7:12 a.m.; the same bedroom; the glass on the nightstand]
The next morning, 7:12. A glass slides off your nightstand.
This time you reach first. You miss. And something catches it anyway.
So if you woke up tomorrow with Star Platinum, would you keep it, or give it back?
And what's the first thing you'd catch? Tell me in the comments.
