## Cold open (0:00)
[11:47 da noite; um quarto; um anel no teu dedo; uma figura enorme e pálida atrás de ti, com uma boca de dentes enormes; cortes rápidos: cinco cartões de cor]
You've woken up with Yuta Okkotsu's Copy, from Jujutsu Kaisen. In real life.
It can copy anything you watch someone do.
And the thing standing behind you decides what it costs.
In forty days, five of the things you've copied will save fourteen people in a flooded tunnel.
But right now it's 11:47 at night, there's a ring on your finger you've never seen, and something huge is breathing behind you.

## 11:47 p.m.: the ring (0:40)
[o quarto; o anel; a figura enorme; a tua mãe a passar no corredor]
In the story, the ring was a promise.
Rika Orimoto was Yuta's friend when they were children. She gave him her mother's ring and promised to marry him. Then she died young.
Yuta couldn't let go. Her soul stayed, tied to his cursed energy, and it became a special-grade curse, the Queen of Curses.
Now it's behind you. Pale, huge, all teeth, and standing very close.
Your mum walks past the door and says goodnight to the wardrobe.
It doesn't attack. It just looks at the ring.
Lesson one: it isn't a weapon. It's a promise with a very big shadow.

## 7:10 a.m.: the omelette (1:30)
[a cozinha; o teu pai a fazer uma omelete; as tuas mãos a copiar]
In the story, Copy lets Yuta reproduce other people's techniques. And nobody is completely sure of the exact conditions.
So, at breakfast, you watch your dad flip an omelette. You've never flipped one in your life.
Your hands do it. Perfectly. A little fold at the end, like he does.
You try the second one on your own, and it's a disaster.
Lesson two: you copy what you see, not what you understand.

## 12:30 p.m.: the busker (2:10)
[uma rua movimentada; um músico de rua; os teus dedos a voar]
At lunch you stop in front of a busker with a guitar.
You watch his hands for one song. Then you borrow the guitar and play his whole piece, note for note, the little slides and all.
A small crowd stops. He stares at you like you've stolen something.
Then your fingers start to shake. They can do it, but they've never done it before, and the body notices.
Lesson three: copying a skill isn't the same as being built for it.

## 6 p.m.: the climbing wall (2:55)
[um ginásio de escalada; a parede; tu a cair; a figura enorme a agarrar-te pelo capuz]
In the evening you watch a climber on a wall, then copy every move.
Halfway up, you realise you copied his reach but not his arms. You slip.
Something huge and pale grabs you by the hood, in mid-air, and sets you down very gently.
It looks at you. It doesn't look pleased.
You never find out if that was a rescue or a warning.
Lesson four: she's not your servant. Sometimes she catches you anyway.

## Day 3: the voice (3:35)
[uma sala de aula; uma apresentação; a turma em silêncio]
On day three, you have to give a presentation at work.
In the film, Yuta uses Cursed Speech, the technique that belongs to Toge Inumaki's family. It's normally passed down only inside that clan.
You don't have that. But you've copied your old teacher's voice, the one that made a whole classroom go quiet.
You open your mouth, and the room stops.
It's a little too good. A colleague goes pale and asks you not to do that again.
Lesson five: when you copy a voice, you copy what it does to other people.

## Day 6: the habit (4:10)
[um café; um amigo a olhar-te; a tua mão a bater na mesa]
On day six, your friend says something odd. You've started talking like the man who shouted at you on the bus.
You didn't decide it. You just watched him, and it stuck.
In 1961, the psychologist Albert Bandura showed that children who watched an adult hit an inflatable doll later did the same, without being told to.
We copy more than we think.
You count how many strangers you've copied this week. You stop at twenty.
Lesson six: you don't choose everything you copy.

## Day 10: the world notices (4:50)
[um parque; um telemóvel a filmar; três competências diferentes no mesmo dia]
A boy films you in the park: first juggling, then a handstand, then a perfect cartwheel.
He posts it. People say it's editing. People say you're hiding something.
Nobody says "Rika", because nobody knows who that is, and the few who do think it's a joke.
Lesson seven: the ones who'd understand are the ones who'd never believe it.

## Day 15: the grandmaster (5:20)
[um parque; um tabuleiro de xadrez; um mestre idoso]
On day fifteen, an old chess player lets you sit down across from him.
You copy his opening, move for move. It's beautiful.
Then he makes a move you've never seen, and you don't know why. You lose in eleven turns.
He smiles. "You have my hands," he says. "You don't have my reasons."
Lesson eight: Copy shows you the move. It never shows you the why.

## Day 20: the ring (6:00)
[a tua casa, à noite; tu a tirar o anel; a luz a tremer; a figura enorme a crescer]
On day twenty you take the ring off, to see what happens.
In the story, Yuta calls Rika out using that ring. And her cursed energy is vast.
The lights go out. The room gets very cold. Something huge unfolds behind you, taller than the ceiling, and the walls start to hum.
You put the ring back on.
It all folds away, slowly. She never moved from where she was.
Lesson nine: the ring isn't a leash. It's a door, and you're the only one who can close it.

## Day 30: the cost (6:45)
[o teu quarto; tu, sentado no chão; os cartões de cor à tua volta]
By day thirty you have a hundred skills in your hands, and not one you can explain.
You're tired in a way sleep doesn't fix. You can do anything you've watched, and you've forgotten how to do the things you used to love.
You find your old guitar. You can't play it any more. Only the busker's song.
Lesson ten: you can borrow a lot, but you can't borrow a self.

## Day 40: the tunnel (7:25)
[uma estrada; um túnel inundado; um autocarro parado; catorze pessoas; chuva]
On day forty, the rain is so heavy that an underpass floods around a stopped bus, with fourteen people inside.
You're the only one who can do anything.
You have a hundred skills in your hands. You need five.
The swimmer's stroke, to get across. The climber's grip, to reach the roof. The driver's calm, to open the door. The locksmith's fingers, for the jammed window. The strongman's back, to hold it.
You put the ring on your finger and say nothing.
Something huge and pale rises behind you, and the water goes very still.
For the first time, it doesn't look at the ring. It looks at the bus.
You get them out one by one. Fourteen people on a wet pavement, shaking.
Nobody asks what you are.

## Day 41: the quiet (8:50)
[o teu quarto; a figura enorme sentada ao pé da cama; tu, exausto]
The next morning you can't lift your arms, and she's sitting at the foot of your bed.
In the story, after the film, Rika's soul moves on, and Yuta keeps using her power.
Yours doesn't move on. She stays.
You say thank you, out loud, to a wardrobe, and for a moment the ring feels warm.
Lesson eleven: the strongest thing you copied wasn't a skill. It was a promise to keep going.

The next night, at 11:47, you look at the ring on your finger.
