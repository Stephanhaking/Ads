## Cold open (0:00)
[Segunda-feira, 7:12 da manhã; uma cozinha; um pão de forma na bancada; um dedo de mão a apontar]
You've woken up as Ryomen Sukuna, the King of Curses. In real life.
You have seven days. At the end of the seventh, nobody in range survives.
[CANON] In the story, Sukuna was a human sorcerer, so feared in the Heian era that nobody could stop him.
[OUR RULE] In your version, you didn't eat a finger. You woke up with all of it, and a clock.
At midnight on day seven, the biggest thing he can do opens on its own. It will not ask who is standing nearby.
But first, it's Monday. You're hungry, and there's a loaf of bread on the counter.

## Day 1, Monday: the bread (0:40)
[a bancada; o pão em fatias muito finas; uma linha fina a atravessar o saco, a bancada e a parede]
[CANON] His technique has two parts. Dismantle cuts from a distance. Cleave cuts what it touches.
[CANON] How hard Dismantle hits depends on how much power you put in.
You point two fingers at the bread and think: cut.
Eleven slices. Thin as paper. And a clean line through the bag, the counter and the wall behind it.
Mara, your roommate, walks in with her coffee. "Did you just cut the wall?"
"It was already like that."
"It was not already like that."
[OUR RULE] In your version, there's one dial, and it's stuck on loud. You can't turn it down yet.
Then you see your face in the bathroom mirror. There are dark marks on it that weren't there last night.
[OUR RULE] The marks are the first sign that your body is changing. They'll come back every morning, a little deeper.
And a voice, not loud at all, says: "Good. Now the neighbour's dog."
You say no out loud. Mara asks who you're talking to.
[CANON] In the story, Sukuna is described as sadistic, selfish and hedonistic.
[OUR RULE] In your version, that personality doesn't take over. It suggests. Seven days of suggestions.
Six days left.

## Day 2, Tuesday: the touch (2:35)
[o parque; uma corrente de bicicleta cortada em duas; uma manga de casaco com uma linha limpa]
[CANON] Cleave needs contact, and it adjusts itself to how tough the target is. It doesn't ask how strong the thing is. It just opens it.
Mara's bike lock is steel. She's proud of it. You close your hand around the chain. It parts like licorice.
"That was a ninety-dollar lock."
"It was a bad lock."
"It was a good lock. You're a bad roommate."
[OUR RULE] In your version, touch is a choice. That's the rule you've been counting on.
At the crosswalk, a stranger grabs your sleeve to stop you stepping in front of a bus. A kind man. Your hand moves before you do. You pull away, fast.
There's a clean line down his jacket from the elbow to the wrist. He thinks it tore. He thanks you anyway.
Lesson two: kindness is the most dangerous thing that can happen to you now.
Five days left.

## Day 3, Wednesday: the heal (4:05)
[a cozinha; uma mão com um corte a fechar-se; olheiras]
You cut your own palm with Dismantle by accident, and watch it close in under three seconds.
[CANON] Sukuna can use Reverse Cursed Technique to heal. Almost nobody in the story can.
Mara stares. "Does it hurt?"
"Not anymore."
[OUR RULE] In your version, every heal takes the sleep you would have had. It isn't free. It's borrowed.
By Wednesday night you haven't slept in two days, and you aren't tired.
[OUR GUESS] Our guess is that a human brain can't run this long without rest. The voice gets louder when you're tired. That's a guess, not a fact.
"Your friend is afraid," the voice says. "Fear is a form of respect."
You turn the light off and leave it off. You don't need it.
Across the hall, the neighbour's dog barks at your door all night. The voice hums along with it. At 3 a.m., you find yourself standing in front of that door, with two fingers raised and no memory of crossing the floor.
You go back to bed. You do not sleep. You lie there and promise yourself: tomorrow, we write down every rule.
Mara writes them on the fridge. Rule one: no touching people. Rule two: no touching animals. Rule three: if you start smiling, you say so out loud.
It's a good list. It won't be enough.
Four days left.

## Day 4, Thursday: Open (5:30)
[um parque de estacionamento vazio às 5 da manhã; um poste de betão cortado; uma seta de fogo]
Mara makes you promise: if you test the next one, she's there.
[CANON] The fire technique, Fuga, only works after Dismantle and Cleave. You have to say "Open."
So you cut a concrete post. Then you touch it. Then you say the word.
An arrow of fire crosses the empty lot, a hundred meters, and takes a dumpster with it.
Mara's eyebrows are fine. Mostly.
She says, very quietly: "You laughed."
You hadn't noticed.
[OUR RULE] In your version, that's the line the marks cross on Thursday. They reach your jaw.
The voice says: "Now you understand why I never needed anyone."
[CANON] In the story, Sukuna fights for the sake of the fight. Power isn't a tool to him. It's the point.
[OUR RULE] In your version, that's the temptation. Not the strength. The relief of not needing anybody.
You walk home in silence. Mara walks half a step behind you, the way people walk near a dog they aren't sure about.
Three days left.

## Day 5, Friday: the bus stop (7:00)
[um abrigo de autocarro à noite; um homem a empurrar Mara; dois dedos levantados; um candeeiro a cair]
A man shoves Mara at the bus stop, grabs her phone and laughs.
"Finish him," the voice says. Calm. Almost kind.
Your hand is up before you decide. Two fingers. A clean line already in the air.
You don't throw it. You throw yourself in front of the street lamp instead.
The lamp falls. Everybody runs. The man drops her phone and keeps running.
Nobody is hurt. Mara is shaking.
"I saw your face. For a second you looked happy."
[OUR RULE] In your version, that's the real cost. Not the power. What it makes easy.
That night she doesn't go to her sister's. She sits on the kitchen floor and says: "Tell me what happens on Sunday."
You almost don't tell her. The voice says there's no reason to. Then you look at the list on the fridge. Rule three is still there.
You say: "I'm smiling right now, aren't I?"
"Yes," she says. "Tell me anyway."
Two days left.

## Day 6, Saturday: the map (8:25)
[um mapa no telemóvel; um círculo vermelho; uma pedreira abandonada]
You tell her the truth.
[CANON] The Malevolent Shrine has no barrier. It doesn't close a wall around anyone. It cuts everything in range, Cleave on anything with energy, Dismantle on everything else.
[CANON] The wiki says its reach can stretch to about two hundred meters, using a Binding Vow.
[OUR RULE] In your version, it opens by itself at midnight on Sunday. You can't stop it. You can only choose where.
You need three hundred meters of nothing. You find a quarry, four kilometers out of town, no houses, no road, one gate.
Mara reads the map. "You're going alone."
"That's the plan."
"That's a terrible plan."
You think about calling someone. The police. A doctor. A priest. And saying what? "A fictional king lives in my body, and I need an empty field by midnight"?
So you spend Saturday doing the only thing left. You drive out to the quarry and practice.
You draw a circle in chalk on the gravel. Then a smaller one inside it. You stand at the center and try to feel the edge, the way you feel the edge of a step in the dark.
[OUR GUESS] Our guess is that learning where a power stops is harder than learning how hard it hits. Nobody teaches that part.
By sunset you can hit the edge of the small circle, twice out of five. Not three. Not four.
Twice out of five isn't enough. It's what you have.
One day left.

## Day 7, Sunday: the quarry (9:15)
[uma pedreira de noite; uma lua vermelha; um boneco de pé; uma figura a 150 metros]
11:40 p.m. Your arms are covered in marks. The voice has stopped suggesting. It just talks now, like it's always lived there.
"Let her stay," it says. "Loss teaches better than anything."
You turn around. Mara followed you. She's standing a hundred and fifty meters behind, at the gate, with a flashlight.
Not far enough.
[CANON] In Shibuya, Sukuna narrowed his Shrine to about one hundred and forty meters to spare Megumi.
[OUR RULE] In your version, you can do the same. But a vow isn't free. You give up the thing you're narrowing for.
You say it out loud: the radius, the number and the price. You aren't paying with power. You're paying with the King. All of him. All the marks, all the voice, all the ease.
Midnight.
It opens without a sound. Not a wall. Not a dome. Just everything in range, cut. The quarry wall, the crane, the old sign, the gravel. Thousands of clean lines, then nothing.
Hundred and forty meters. Not a hundred and fifty.
Mara is ten meters outside it. Her flashlight is still on.
The voice says one last thing, very small: "Interesting."
Then it's gone.

## Monday again (9:55)
[a cozinha; o pão a fatias grossas e irregulares; um cartão com uma linha na parede]
Seven days. Nobody in range survived, because there was no one in range. That was the whole plan.
You wake up as yourself. You're lying on the quarry floor at dawn, and Mara is sitting next to you, holding her flashlight, which is finally out of battery.
"You smiled for a second, at the end," she says.
"I know."
"You can stop now."
You laugh, and it's your laugh again.
At home, the marks are gone. The marks are gone. The bread on the counter is a normal loaf, and Mara has already burned a piece of it.
The wall still has its clean line. The landlord still hasn't noticed.
[OUR RULE] In your version, that's what the title meant. Nobody survives seven days as the King. Not even the King.
Would you have narrowed it? Tell me in the comments. And if you want the next one, tell me who you want to become.
