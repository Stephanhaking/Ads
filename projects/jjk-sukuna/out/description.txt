What if you became Sukuna in real life? You get seven days, and at midnight on the seventh, nobody in range survives.

You've woken up as Ryomen Sukuna, the King of Curses from Jujutsu Kaisen, with all of his power and a clock. Over seven days you slice a loaf of bread (and the wall behind it), open your roommate Mara's steel bike lock like licorice, heal a cut in three seconds and stop sleeping, find yourself at the neighbour's door at 3 a.m., fire an arrow of fire across an empty car park, almost answer a man at a bus stop the way the voice wants you to, and drive out to a quarry to learn where the power stops before midnight on Sunday.

WHAT'S LORE AND WHAT'S OURS
In the story (Jujutsu Kaisen, from secondary sources): Sukuna was a human sorcerer of the Heian era; his technique has two parts, Dismantle (cuts from a distance; how hard it hits depends on the energy put in) and Cleave (needs contact and adjusts to how tough the target is); the fire technique Fuga ("Open") only comes after Dismantle and Cleave; he can heal with Reverse Cursed Technique; he is described as sadistic, selfish and hedonistic; his Malevolent Shrine has no barrier and cuts everything in range (Cleave on anything with cursed energy, Dismantle on everything else); the fan wiki gives its reach as up to about 200 meters with a Binding Vow, and about 140 meters in Shibuya, narrowed to spare Megumi.
Our fiction: waking up with all of his power and a seven-day clock, the marks that deepen every morning, the voice that only suggests, the single "loud" setting, heals that cost sleep, the Shrine opening by itself at midnight, the vow that pays with the King himself, and every character and place in the video (Mara, the flat, the park, the crossing, the car park, the bus stop, the quarry). No scientific study is cited; the "tired brain" line is labelled as our guess.
