# What If You Had Star Platinum in Real Life? (Jotaro, JoJo's Bizarre Adventure)
# v01, for approval before voice and visuals. Same style as Luffy/Killua: second person, the power in the first sentence,
# a day counter, one numbered rule per block, real facts inside a scene, the world reacts in the middle, the cost at the end,
# a two-part question.
# Different openings so far: Luffy = a fall on the stairs, JoJo = a phone off a balcony, Death Note = a laundromat,
# Killua = a stuck lift. This one = a tower of tins collapsing in a supermarket.
# Climax = a van with no driver rolling toward a crossing (not a fire, not a ferris wheel).
# Facts only from docs/VERIFICACAO_JOTARO_STARPLATINUM_18NOV.md, "Verificado" rows:
#  - base powers: strength, speed, precision; signature barrage "ORA ORA"; Star Finger stretches two fingers;
#  - Jotaro gets the same time-stop power as The World near the end of Part 3 (about 5 s at the start);
#  - heaviest deadlift 510 kg (Hafthor Bjornsson, 2025); 9 mm bullet about 350 to 410 m/s; boxers' punches about 6 to 11 m/s;
#    reaction time about 190 to 285 ms; Bolt 10.44 m/s average.
#  - our calculations are said as ours: 10 m at 360 m/s = about 28 ms, about nine times shorter than a 250 ms reaction;
#    "twenty punches a second" is only an example.
# Left out: the 2 m range, the 511 kg lift, any claim that the anime gives a bullet-speed number.
# Care: fiction tone, no real weapons on screen and no instructions; the bullet only as a calculation on a whiteboard.
# About 1,170 spoken words, about 7 to 7.5 minutes.

## Cold open (0:00)
[a supermarket on a Saturday morning; a tall pyramid of tinned tomatoes; a trolley clips it]
It's Saturday morning in the supermarket, and you've just woken up with Star Platinum, Jotaro's Stand from JoJo's Bizarre Adventure.
You don't know that yet.
A kid's trolley clips the bottom of a tower of tinned tomatoes, two metres tall, and the whole thing starts to fall.
You flinch.
And something flinches with you. A huge pale arm comes out of your shoulder, blue and purple, faster than your own hand, and catches the first tin. Then the next. Then all of them.
When you open your eyes, forty tins are stacked back on the shelf. Perfectly straight. Labels facing out.
The kid stares at you. His mum stares at the tins. You walk to the till without your shopping.
Over the next forty days you're going to find out what it means to own the fastest pair of hands in the world, and why the fast part is the easy part.

## Day 1: what it is (0:55)
[your bedroom; you, and a tall figure standing behind you, half see-through]
In the story, this kind of power is called a Stand.
Jotaro's is Star Platinum, and its base powers are simple: strength, speed and precision, all far beyond a human.
You test it in front of the mirror. You lift your arm. It lifts its arm.
You think "left", and it's already moved.
In your version, nobody else seems to see it. Your mum walks in and asks why you're staring at the wardrobe.
Rule one: you're not stronger. Something next to you is.

## Day 3: precision (1:30)
[the kitchen; a needle and thread; a falling glass]
You start small. You thread a needle without looking.
You drop a glass on purpose, and it's back on the table before it gets to your knees.
Here's what you're up against. Human visual reaction time is about a quarter of a second, somewhere between one hundred and ninety and two hundred and eighty-five milliseconds.
Everything you've ever caught, you caught after that delay.
Star Platinum doesn't seem to have one. It moves inside the gap between seeing and doing.
Rule two: precision beats strength. You never break a single glass again.

## Day 6: strength (2:10)
[a gym at 6 a.m.; one bar loaded with every plate in the building]
At six in the morning, you take Star Platinum to an empty gym.
The heaviest deadlift ever recorded is five hundred and ten kilograms, lifted by Hafthor Bjornsson in twenty twenty-five. That's a human at the absolute edge.
You load every plate in the building onto one bar.
Star Platinum picks it up with one hand, like a shopping bag.
The bar bends in the middle. The owner walks in, looks at the bent bar, looks at you, and asks you to leave.
Rule three: strength is easy. Putting things down gently is the hard part.

## Day 10: the barrage (2:50)
[a boxing gym; a heavy bag; then sand all over the floor]
Every fan knows what comes next. The barrage. ORA ORA ORA.
So you try it on a heavy bag.
Real boxers' punches move at roughly six to eleven metres per second.
Now say, just as an example, that a Stand throws twenty punches a second at that speed. Twenty times the work, every second, for as long as you shout.
The bag lasts four seconds. Then it splits, and there's sand all over the gym.
You sit down on the floor and you're starving. Not hungry. Starving. You eat three dinners that night.
Rule four: every punch has to be paid for, and you're the one who pays.

## Day 15: the world notices (3:35)
[a park; a kid with a phone; a blurry video]
A boy in the park films you catching his football before it hits a pram.
The video is blurry. You're standing still, and the ball just stops in the air, a metre from the baby.
Some people say it's edited. Some people say it's a ghost.
Nobody says "Stand", because nobody knows what that is, except the people who've read the manga, and they think it's a joke.
Rule five: the only people who'd understand are the ones who'd never believe it.

## Day 18: Star Finger (4:10)
[a street at night; your keys falling through a drain grate]
At night your keys fall through a drain grate.
In the story, Star Platinum has a move called Star Finger: it stretches two of its fingers to reach further than its arms can.
You try it. Two fingers slide down between the bars like a pair of chopsticks, and come back up with your keys, and a two-euro coin, and somebody's wedding ring.
You hand the ring in at the police station. You don't explain how you got it.
Rule six: the useful tricks are the small ones.

## Day 22: how fast is fast enough (4:45)
[a whiteboard in your room; numbers, no weapons]
Fans love the scenes where Star Platinum catches bullets. So you start asking how fast is fast enough.
You don't test it. You never would. You do it on a whiteboard.
A nine-millimetre pistol bullet leaves the barrel at around three hundred and fifty to four hundred and ten metres per second.
Our own maths: at three hundred and sixty metres per second, it crosses ten metres in about twenty-eight milliseconds.
Your reaction time is about two hundred and fifty. That's nine times too slow, before you've even decided to move.
Usain Bolt, the fastest human ever measured, averaged about ten metres per second.
You put the marker down. Some things you don't need to know.
Rule seven: being fast doesn't make you fast enough for everything.

## Day 30: the job nobody asked you to do (5:25)
[a café; a tray of glasses tipping; you catching it without looking up]
By day thirty you can't stop catching things.
Falling cups. Falling phones. A tray of glasses in a café. A toddler falling off a wall.
You start walking around with your shoulders tight, waiting for the next thing to fall.
Your friends notice you never relax. You watch the edges of every table.
Rule eight: when you can catch everything, you start feeling responsible for everything.

## Day 40: the van (5:55)
[a hill; a delivery van with nobody inside, rolling; a school crossing at the bottom]
On day forty, a delivery van rolls down a hill with nobody in it.
At the bottom there's a school crossing, and the lollipop lady has just stepped out.
You run. You're not going to make it. Star Platinum isn't going to make it either.
Then the world goes silent.
In the story, near the end of Part three, Jotaro finds out his Stand has the same power as The World: it can stop time, for about five seconds at first.
The van hangs in the air like a photograph. The birds stop mid-wing.
One second: you reach the van. Two: Star Platinum puts both hands on the bonnet. Three: it turns the van toward a hedge. Four: you step back.
Five.
The van hits the hedge at walking speed. Nobody is hurt.
The lollipop lady blinks at you, and keeps holding her sign.

## Day 41: the cost (6:45)
[your room; the figure behind you; you, very tired]
The next morning your hands are shaking, and they don't stop until lunch.
You've learned what it really is: not a superpower, a second body that never gets tired, attached to one that does.
Everything it does, you feel afterwards.
Rule nine: the strongest thing about you is still the most tired thing about you.

So if you woke up tomorrow with Star Platinum, would you keep it, or give it back?
And what's the first thing you'd catch?
