What If You Had The World in Real Life? (JoJo — stopping time)

Your phone slips off a balcony twelve floors up, and it stops in mid-air. So does everything else. Over 40 days you learn what five seconds of frozen time is really worth: a silent city, a frisbee that jumps, a running track, a video that looks like teleporting, a casino you walk past, people in grey suits with stopwatches, a fire you fight nine seconds at a time, and the cost of living moments nobody else lives.

THE REAL SCIENCE AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
- Sound needs air to travel; in air it moves at about 343 m/s, passed from molecule to molecule
- Air molecules themselves move at about 500 m/s on average at room temperature (HyperPhysics)
- A person needs about a quarter of a second to react to something they see (reaction-time studies)
- Usain Bolt ran 100 m in 9.58 s (2009), an average of about 10.4 m/s; in five seconds even he covers only about 52 m (our calculation)
- In 2013, physicists at TU Darmstadt held light still inside a crystal for up to a minute and stored an image in it (Physical Review Letters 111, 033601)
