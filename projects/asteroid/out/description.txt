What If an Asteroid Was Heading for Earth?

The "star" Kai has been watching every night is not a star. It's a rock about 150 metres wide, and it's coming. In Bo's garage, the five friends find out what that really means: how fast asteroids hit, how much energy they carry, what small, medium and giant rocks have already done to Earth, and how humans already moved one on purpose.

The story is fiction: no real asteroid is known to be on a collision course. The science and the history are real (sources below).

THE SCIENCE AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
- Asteroids typically hit Earth at about 20 km/s (impact-modelling studies)
- A 150 m rock at 20 km/s carries roughly 170 megatons of TNT (our estimate: 1/2 m v^2, density 2,000 kg/m^3); the biggest bomb ever tested was about 50 Mt
- 15 Feb 2013, Chelyabinsk, Russia: a rock about 17-19 m across exploded in the air with roughly 30 times the energy of the Hiroshima bomb; about 1,500 people were hurt, mostly by glass from blown-in windows (Wikipedia; AP; CBC)
- 30 June 1908, Tunguska, Siberia: an air burst flattened about 80 million trees over about 2,150 km^2 (Wikipedia; Britannica)
- Meteor Crater, Arizona: about 1.2 km wide, made about 50,000 years ago by an iron meteorite about 50 m across (NASA Earth Observatory; Arizona State University)
- About 66 million years ago, a rock about 10 km across hit what is now Mexico (Chicxulub studies)
- 13 April 2029: asteroid Apophis (about 340 m) passes about 32,000 km from Earth, inside the ring of geostationary satellites, and will not hit (ESA; The Planetary Society)
- NASA's NEO Surveyor infrared telescope, launching no earlier than 2027, aims to find most near-Earth objects larger than 140 m (NASA JPL)
- 26 Sept 2022: NASA's DART spacecraft hit Dimorphos (about 150 m) at about 22,500 km/h and shortened its orbit by about 32 minutes (Science News; Space.com)

These figures come from secondary sources and search summaries; not every original paper was read. If you spot an error, tell us in the comments.
