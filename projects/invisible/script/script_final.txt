So why aren't we see-through already? A glass of water is. Water is the same all the way through, so light passes straight across it. But your body is a mix. Water, fat and proteins all bend light by slightly different amounts. Every time light crosses from one to the next, it bends a bit. After millions of tiny bends, it's scattered everywhere. And you look solid.

Some animals have figured this out. Meet the glass frog. Its skin and muscles are nearly see-through. The problem is blood. Red blood is very easy to see. So when it sleeps, the glass frog hides almost ninety percent of its red blood cells inside its liver. That makes it two to three times more transparent. Scientists still aren't sure how it does this without its blood clotting.

And in 2024, scientists at Stanford did something wild with food colouring. They rubbed a yellow food dye on the skin of a living mouse. And the skin turned see-through.
