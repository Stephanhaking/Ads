What If You Had the Death Note in Real Life?

At eleven at night, in an empty laundromat, you find a black notebook on top of the lost-and-found box. The first rule says the person whose name is written in it will die. Over 31 days you never write a name, but someone else does: heart attacks on the news, a pattern, a detective watching it, a shinigami with an offer, a fake rule designed to scare you into using it, and one rooftop where you finally have to decide.

This is a fan-made "what if" story. THE LORE (secondary sources)
- In the story, a name written with no cause means a heart attack about 40 seconds later; if a cause is written, there are 6 minutes 40 seconds to add the details (CBR)
- The writer must have the person's face in mind (CBR, Death Note Wiki)
- Shinigami eyes cost half of the owner's remaining life, and each new deal halves it again (Death Note Wiki, Wikipedia)
- The "13-day rule" is fake: in the story, Light invents it to mislead the police (CBR)

THE REAL WORLD AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
- Harold Shipman (UK): the inquiry first concluded he killed 215 people, and later estimated about 250 (Wikipedia, Britannica)
- The first suspicions came from other doctors, who noticed his patients were dying unusually often; statisticians later argued that monitoring death rates would have caught him years earlier (Wikipedia)
- Homicide clearance: about 95% in Japan (2008 study, cited by the National Police Agency) and about 52% in the United States in 2023 (Bureau of Justice Statistics). The two are not counted the same way.
