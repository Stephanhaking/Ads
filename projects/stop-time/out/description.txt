What If You Could Stop Time?

A dodgeball flies at your face, you think one word, "Stop", and the whole world freezes. This story follows what stopping time would really be like: total silence, air as solid as a wall, darkness, and a breath you can't refill. Plus the real experiments where scientists actually stopped light.

The story and the "time bubble" are fiction (no known physics allows stopping time). The science around it is real (sources below).

THE SCIENCE AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
- Sound is a pressure wave carried by air molecules (about 343 m/s in dry air); with no moving air there is no sound
- Air molecules at room temperature move at roughly 500 m/s (nitrogen rms ≈ 515 m/s at 300 K; kinetic theory)
- Physicists note that pushing through truly frozen air would take enormous force (popular physics write-ups; treat as a thought experiment)
- To see, light (photons) must reach your retina; a philosopher of physics, Craig Callender, told Live Science a time-stopper probably couldn't see (Live Science, "Can time stop?")
- Breath-holding: most untrained people manage roughly 30–90 s; the record without pure oxygen is 11 min 35 s (Stéphane Mifsud); brain damage can begin after about 4 minutes without oxygen (MedlinePlus via Live Science)
- 2013, TU Darmstadt (Thomas Halfmann's team): light stopped for about one minute in a praseodymium-doped crystal, storing a simple image of three stripes (ScienceDaily, Phys.org)
- 2018, Caltech (Lihong Wang's team): the T-CUP camera filmed at ten trillion frames per second (Kavli Foundation, Laser Focus World)
- Chronostasis, the stopped-clock illusion: after a quick glance at a clock, the first second can seem longer (Yarrow et al., Nature 2001)

These figures come from secondary sources and search summaries; not every original paper was read. If you spot an error, tell us in the comments.
