What If You Had Megumi's Ten Shadows in Real Life? (Jujutsu Kaisen)

At 3:15 in the morning your shadow stops copying you. Then two dogs step out of it, one white and one black. Over 40 days you find out what it really means to carry the Ten Shadows in real life: a toad in the office fridge, an elephant in an underground car park, a white serpent filling your hallway, a fusion that goes wrong, a garden of shadow on a football pitch at night, a lost child in the forest, and a car park that collapses with eleven people underneath it. And there's one thing in your shadow you should never open.

WHAT'S LORE AND WHAT'S REAL
In the story (Jujutsu Kaisen): the Ten Shadows Technique; the Divine Dogs (one white, one black) as the first shikigami; Toad, and that two shikigami can fuse; the Great Serpent; Max Elephant and its cost in cursed energy; Mahoraga, the wheel that turns as it adapts, the ritual that only ends with the target's death or with it tamed, and that only Sukuna ever tamed it; Chimera Shadow Garden, a Domain Expansion with no closed barrier that is incomplete.
Real world: an adult African elephant (average male) weighs about 6 tonnes; a dog can have up to about 300 million scent receptors against about 6 million in a human (upper limit, best case); the longest wild snake ever measured is a 7.22 m reticulated python (Guinness World Records, February 2026).
Our own maths: 6,000 kg × 9.8 m/s² × 3 m ≈ 176 kJ.
The scenes in the world (the city at night, the cat, the office, the car park, the lost child, the man in the suit) are our own fiction, not part of the story.
