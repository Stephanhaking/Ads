## Cold open (0:00)
[um quarto às 3:15 da manhã; a lua na janela; a tua sombra no chão fica parada quando te mexes; cortes: lobos de sombra, elefante, serpente]
You've woken up with Megumi's Ten Shadows, from Jujutsu Kaisen. In real life.
Your shadow is alive. And it's hungry.
One of the things inside it will help you save a car park full of people.
Another has never been tamed by anyone but Sukuna, and in forty days you'll be tempted to open it.
But right now it's 3:15 in the morning, the moon is on your floor, and your shadow has just stopped copying you.

## 3:15 a.m.: the dogs (0:40)
[o quarto; a sombra do armário; dois cães, um branco e um preto, sentam-se]
You lift your arm. The shadow on the floor doesn't.
Then two dogs step out of it, one white and one black, and sit in front of you like they've been waiting all night.
In the story these are the Divine Dogs, the first shikigami every user of the Ten Shadows Technique receives. They hunt curses.
Your cat, who respects nothing, leaves the room.
Then they catch a scent and drag you out of the flat, down the stairs, across the empty city. A dog can have up to about three hundred million scent receptors, against about six million in a human. That's the best case, but it explains why they're not slowing down.
They stop under a rooftop. On it, there's your neighbour's lost cat. It looks at you. It's not impressed either.
Lesson one: your shadow has a perfect nose, and no idea what's yours.

## 7:30 a.m.: breakfast (1:40)
[a cozinha; a tua mãe; a sombra sob a mesa]
At breakfast you try to act normal. Your mum asks why you're sitting so far from the table.
The dogs are under it. You can feel them breathing.
Every time she moves, they move with her shadow, and you realise something: they're not following her. They're guarding you.
You eat a whole bowl of cereal with your feet pulled up on the chair.
Lesson two: they obey you. But they don't understand "normal".

## 12:30 p.m.: the toad (2:10)
[o escritório; a copa; um sapo do tamanho de uma pessoa]
At lunch you try the next one, as quietly as you can.
Something wet slaps out of your shadow and onto the office floor: a toad the size of a person.
In the story this is Toad, another shikigami. And in the story, two of them can even fuse into something new.
You hide it in the kitchen fridge. It leaves a tongue's worth of mess on the printer.
Your boss asks who's been feeding the printer.
Lesson three: these things don't come with a manual, and they don't ask permission to appear.

## 6 p.m.: the elephant (2:55)
[uma garagem subterrânea vazia; um elefante a sair do chão]
In the evening you take it all to an empty underground car park, because you want to know where your limit is.
In the story, Max Elephant takes a huge amount of cursed energy to summon.
An adult African elephant weighs about six tonnes.
Our own maths: drop six thousand kilograms from three metres, and you get roughly one hundred and seventy-six thousand joules.
You summon it. You make it stand up. Then your legs go, and you sit on the concrete, shaking, because it took everything you had just to hold it there.
You spend an hour finding where you left your keys.
Lesson four: the bigger the shadow, the bigger the bill, and you're the one who pays.

## Day 3: the serpent (3:50)
[o corredor do prédio; uma serpente branca com marcas pretas]
On day three, you try the Great Serpent, a big white snake with black marks across its body.
The longest wild snake ever measured is a reticulated python of seven point two two metres, confirmed by Guinness in twenty twenty-six.
Yours fills the hallway. You can't tell where it ends.
Your neighbour opens her door, looks at the white coil blocking the stairs, and goes back inside without a word.
She never mentions it again, even when you meet in the lift.
Lesson five: when something is bigger than your building, you have to learn to put it back.

## Day 6: the fusion (4:30)
[a escada; dois vultos que se misturam]
In the story, two shikigami can fuse into something new. So, of course, you try it.
You call two at once. They don't merge. They tangle.
For ten seconds there's a creature in your stairwell that has too many legs and the wrong number of opinions about it.
You manage to send them back, but it takes everything you have. You spend the next day in bed.
Lesson six: more is not stronger. More is just more.

## Day 10: the world notices (5:05)
[um parque ao pôr do sol; um miúdo com um telemóvel; a tua sombra com orelhas]
A boy in the park films your shadow at sunset. It's the wrong shape. It has ears, and it's wagging.
The video spreads. Half the comments say it's CGI. The other half say it's a ghost dog.
Nobody says "shikigami", because only a few people know what that means, and they think it's a joke.
Lesson seven: the people who'd understand are the ones who'd never believe it.

## Day 12: the garden (5:35)
[um campo de futebol à noite; a sombra a espalhar-se pelo chão; os cães a correr; tu, ofegante]
On day twelve you take it to an empty football pitch, at night, to try something bigger.
In the story, Megumi's version of a Domain Expansion is called Chimera Shadow Garden. It has no walls, no closed barrier. It spreads his shadow over whatever is already there. And it's incomplete.
So you push your shadow outwards, across the grass, until the whole pitch is dark under your feet.
The dogs run through it faster than anything you've ever seen, as if the ground itself was helping them.
But nothing hits for you. Nothing wins for you. It's only a place where everything you call comes out easier.
You hold it for a minute, and your head feels like it's full of wet sand.
Lesson eight: the garden doesn't fight. It just makes the fight yours.

## Day 15: the man in the suit (6:25)
[um café; um homem de fato cinzento com uma pasta]
A man in a grey suit sits down at your table in a café and doesn't ask a single question.
He slides a card across. He's not interested in what you can do. He wants to know what you'd refuse to do.
You say you don't know yet.
He nods, as if that was the right answer, and leaves without his coffee.
Lesson nine: the first question anyone asks about power is how far you'd go.

## Day 20: the one that stirs (6:55)
[a tua sombra; uma roda de oito lâminas a girar lentamente; tu, imóvel]
On day twenty something stirs in your shadow that isn't a dog, or a toad, or an elephant.
In the story, it's called Mahoraga, the strongest shikigami of the technique. It adapts to whatever hits it, and the wheel on its back turns as it learns.
And the ritual to summon it doesn't end until either its target is dead, or it's tamed. In the story, the only one who ever tamed it was Sukuna.
You close your eyes. The wheel stops turning.
You don't tell anyone.
Lesson ten: there's one door in your shadow you can't walk back through.

## Day 25: the lost child (7:40)
[uma floresta à noite; lanternas; uma criança perdida]
On day twenty-five, a child goes missing at the edge of the city, and the search party is walking in lines with torches.
You stand at the treeline with your hand over your shadow.
The dogs take the scent from the child's coat, and they go. White and black, low through the trees, silent.
They find her in forty minutes, asleep under a fallen trunk, with a muddy shoe in her arms.
Nobody asks how. They're too busy hugging her.
Lesson eleven: sometimes the point isn't what you can do. It's what you do with it.

## Day 30: the cost (8:25)
[a tua casa; sentado no chão; as sombras à volta]
By day thirty you can feel exactly how much each of them costs.
The dogs are cheap. The toad is cheap. The elephant leaves you in bed for a day.
You're treating your own shadow like a bank account, and you're afraid to check the balance.
Lesson twelve: you're never out of shadows. You're only ever out of you.

## Day 40: the car park (8:50)
[o desabamento de um parque de estacionamento; pessoas presas; poeiras; a lua]
On day forty, at night, a floor of the car park near your flat collapses with eleven people underneath it.
You run. You're the only one who can do anything.
There's one thing in your shadow that could lift the slab with one hand. You can feel it turning.
You stand at the edge of the rubble with your hand over your shadow, and you take it away.
So you do the other thing. You spread your shadow across the floor, the way you practised on the pitch, over whatever's already there.
It doesn't need to be perfect. It only needs to be big enough.
The dogs find them by smell, under the concrete. The toad pulls them out, one by one, with its tongue. The serpent slides under the beams. The elephant stands up under the slab and holds it, and your legs shake the whole time.
Eleven people come out. Nobody asks what you are.

## Day 41: the cost, again (10:00)
[o teu quarto; a sombra imóvel; tu, exausto]
The next morning you can't get out of bed.
Now you know what it really is. Not a gift. A door in the floor, and you're the only one who decides what comes through.
Lesson thirteen: the strongest thing in your shadow is the one you didn't open.

## Ending (10:20)
[3:15 a.m.; o quarto; a lua; a tua sombra no chão, parada]
The next night at 3:15, you lift your arm, and your shadow doesn't move.
You wait. Something stirs under it, and it sits. It's the white dog.
So if you woke up tomorrow with Megumi's Ten Shadows, which one would you call first?
And would you ever open the one that can only be stopped by killing you? Tell me in the comments.
