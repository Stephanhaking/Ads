What If You Were Super Strong?

7 a.m. at the harbour, and one of the shipping containers is walking. Underneath it is Bo, and she's carrying it like a backpack. This story looks at what super strength would really take: the strongest humans alive, a beetle that pulls more than a thousand times its own weight, the square-cube law Galileo worked out, and why your bones would snap long before your muscles gave up.

The story (and Bo's power) is fiction. The science and the records are real (sources below).

THE SCIENCE AND WHERE IT COMES FROM
- Heaviest deadlift: 501 kg, Hafthor Julius Bjornsson, Iceland, 2 May 2020 (Guinness World Records); about seven 70 kg adults (our calculation)
- An empty 20-foot shipping container weighs about 2.2 tonnes; loaded, up to about 24-30 tonnes (industry specifications)
- The dung beetle Onthophagus taurus can pull about 1,141 times its body weight, like a 70 kg person pulling 80 tonnes (Knell & Simmons, Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 2010; Queen Mary University of London)
- The square-cube law: double the size and strength (cross-section) goes up 4 times, weight (volume) 8 times; Galileo, Two New Sciences, 1638 (Scientific American)
- The femur's compressive strength is about 205 MPa; it is often said to hold about 30 times body weight when loaded straight down (Body Physics, Open Oregon textbook)

These figures come from secondary sources and search summaries; not every original paper was read. If you spot an error, tell us in the comments.
