# Science ledger: What if you could read minds?

Premise (fiction, story only): the stranger can hear other people's thoughts. Everything below is real neuroscience.
Inputs come from web-search summaries (secondary sources); originals not all opened.

| ID | Quantity | Value | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | Neurons | about 86 billion neurons; roughly 100 trillion synapses (estimates vary) | UCLA Brain Research Institute; Herculano-Houzel 2009 | |
| M02 | EEG signal size | about 50 microvolts at the scalp (vs about a millivolt for the heart's ECG); amplified thousands of times | HyperPhysics "EEG"; Lancaster glossary | |
| M03 | Brain magnetic field | femtotesla range, more than a billion times weaker than Earth's field (one researcher, Oldenburg); MEG needs supercooled sensors in shielded rooms | Univ. Oldenburg news; Karolinska NatMEG | other review says up to 100,000x; use "about a billion" with "one researcher" |
| M04 | Semantic decoder | UT Austin (Tang, LeBel, Jain, Huth), Nature Neuroscience, May 2023: fMRI + language model turned brain activity into text; about 16 hours of podcasts per person to train; gets the gist, not word for word | NIH Research Matters; KEYE / syndicated | |
| M05 | Decoder limits | a decoder trained on one person failed on another; participants could sabotage it by counting, naming animals or thinking of another story; "nobody's brain should be decoded without permission" | NIH; AuntMinnie; Univ. Sydney | |
| M06 | Speech implant | UCSF (Edward Chang), Nature, Aug 2023: Ann, paralysed by a brain-stem stroke 18 years earlier, spoke through an avatar at about 78 words per minute (about 25 % word errors); natural speech about 160 wpm | Scientific American; Inverse; UTEC summary | |
| M07 | Neuralink | March 2024: first patient (Noland Arbaugh, paralysed below the shoulders) moved a cursor and played online chess using the implant; experts called it early days | Reuters / AP via Inquirer, 6abc | |

## Unknowns
- U01 Reading thoughts from a distance, without a scanner or implant: no known physics allows it; signals are tiny and fade fast with distance.
